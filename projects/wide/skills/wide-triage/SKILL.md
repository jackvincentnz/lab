# WIDE triage

Choose the next valuable human action or decision in refinement. This report writes nothing.

1. Read strategy and apply conversational focus or constraints. If strategy is absent, state the gap.
2. Browse compact problem and candidate results with `wide_search`, paging until covered at current scale.
   Browse signals for the requested window, otherwise since the last triage or the past seven days;
   use capture times and read older material when relevant. Search can narrow discovery, but keyword
   matches must not become permanent exclusions. Consider unresolved older opportunities even without
   new signals. This experiment samples a few engineering areas; consider others when signals arrive.
3. Identify promising opportunities, including poorly understood ideas. Get full records and material
   sources for those that could change the recommendation. Inspect their questions, answers, decisions,
   and related options. Inspect both candidates in full before consequential comparisons; reuse full
   material already in context. Previews can omit decisive facts. Expand reading when uncertainty could
   change the recommendation; disclose scanned versus examined records and incomplete coverage.
4. Compare the value of the opportunity and, separately, the value of refining it now. Name the decision
   that learning would improve. Check existing work before proposing new investigation. Cheap checks,
   routine-work labels, and repeated mentions don't establish impact. Unsupported cost estimates and
   outcome predictions are hypotheses; an evidence result may inform a choice without determining it.
   Distinguish active effort from delay, verify frequency and source scope, and don't generalize causes
   from one sample. Historical analysis isn't independent corroboration.
5. Recommend one refinement action and up to two alternatives, with IDs, potential value, learning effort,
   uncertainty, and why the recommendation deserves attention. End with one bounded question or evidence
   action and how different answers could change a decision. Keep supporting groupings brief. Further
   evidence, parking, or no refinement now can be valid recommendations; don't invent scores or stages.

Carry forward human decisions and settled answers; explain any reason to revisit them. Surface credible
urgency for human attention without authorizing implementation. Subsequent user-directed intake or
refinement can save new understanding; producing this report itself does not.
